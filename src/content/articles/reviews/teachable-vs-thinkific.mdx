Most Teachable vs Thinkific comparisons ask which platform has more features. That's the wrong question for anyone who builds courses for a living. Both can host video, sell a course and send a certificate. The useful question is what each one is built to do well, and what it costs to get the features that matter for learning.

Our short answer: Teachable is a creator-commerce platform. It makes the business of selling a course simple. Thinkific is a learning-delivery platform. It gives you more of the tools that make a course teach. Which is better depends on whether your course is a product you sell or a learning experience you run.

## How we built this comparison

This comparison is built from evidence, and we label what kind each claim is:

- **Vendor documentation:** what Teachable and Thinkific say their products do, from their current pricing, plan, security and accessibility pages, checked on 7 November 2026.
- **Customer reviews:** how users on review sites describe their experience. We treat these as indicative, not representative.
- **Assurance documentation:** what the vendors publish about security, accessibility and data hosting. A published statement isn't the same as an independent audit.

We haven't yet scored either platform on our seven-criterion [review method](/how-we-review/). Those scores come from structured hands-on testing, which is under way, and we'll add them here when it's done. Until then, this page makes no 1 to 5 judgements.

## Choose Teachable if… choose Thinkific if…

**Choose Teachable if** you're an independent creator, coach or subject expert who wants to package what you know into paid courses, memberships or downloads, and the commercial side matters most: checkout, tax handling, upsells and affiliates.

**Choose Thinkific if** you run a training business, a professional association or a provider whose learners need structure: assignments, certificates, completion rules and a community around the course.

**Look beyond both if** you're a university or a large organisation that needs institutional identity management, student-system integration, formal assessment moderation or Australian data residency. Neither platform is a drop-in replacement for an institutional learning management system (LMS).

## Quizzes and practice

Practice that makes learners recall what they've learnt is one of the best-supported ideas in learning research, so the quiz tools matter more than they look. Our guide to [writing multiple-choice questions](/guides/writing-multiple-choice-questions/) covers what good practice questions look like.

**Teachable** includes auto-graded multiple-choice quizzes on every plan ([Teachable, 2025](https://support.teachable.com/en/articles/11682410-new-teachable-plans-in-june-2025)). Its course compliance settings, also on every plan, can require learners to complete a graded quiz before moving on, with a minimum passing score and up to 99 retakes ([Teachable course compliance](https://intercom.help/teachableknowledgebase/en/articles/11682463-course-compliance)). [TO VERIFY: whether Teachable quizzes support question types beyond multiple choice, question banks or randomisation]

**Thinkific** includes quizzes on every plan, from Start ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)). Exams, which add attempt limits and passing scores in a distraction-free exam environment, are reserved for Thinkific Plus.

Both handle the basics: a short graded quiz at the end of each lesson. For a high-stakes final assessment, Thinkific's exam tools are more complete, but only on its enterprise tier.

## Assignments and feedback

Feedback on real work is where a course moves from content to teaching. Our library entry on [effective feedback](/library/effective-feedback/) explains why.

**Thinkific** lists assignments on every plan, alongside quizzes and certificates, described as a way to "grade student work" ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)). One reviewer quoted in our research chose Thinkific because "I did not want to just 'sell online courses.' I wanted to truly teach those that bought my courses", and singled out homework submissions, drip schedules and prerequisites ([G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/thinkific/reviews/)).

**Teachable's** plan documentation doesn't list assignments or graded written submissions. [TO VERIFY: whether Teachable offers assignments or file submissions, and on which plan]

On the evidence available, Thinkific has the clearer offer here.

## Certificates and credentials

This is the sharpest difference between the two on price.

**Thinkific** includes completion certificates on every plan, starting with Start at US$82 a month billed annually ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)).

**Teachable** lists course completion certificates only on its Growth plan and above ([Teachable, 2025](https://support.teachable.com/en/articles/11682410-new-teachable-plans-in-june-2025)). Growth costs US$139 a month billed annually, or US$189 monthly ([Teachable pricing](https://teachable.com/pricing)).

So the cheapest way to issue a certificate is Thinkific, by US$57 a month on annual billing. Neither platform documents verifiable digital credentials, such as Open Badges, as a built-in feature. If your learners need credentials an employer can check, plan for a separate credentialing tool. Customer feedback in our research also points to limits on certificate customisation in Thinkific, including assigning more than one certificate to a course.

## Learner analytics

What you can see about your learners decides whether you can improve a course. Separate two kinds of reporting: commercial (sales, conversion, refunds) and learning (progress, completion, quiz results, where learners stop).

**Thinkific** includes standard reporting on every plan, adds fuller reporting and analytics, including student engagement reports that show "where learners get stuck", from Grow (US$164 a month billed annually), and keeps advanced reporting and custom dashboards for Plus ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)).

**Teachable** includes reporting, but its plan documentation doesn't break down what learning data each plan shows. [TO VERIFY: Teachable's learner progress and quiz reporting, and whether it differs by plan] In customer reviews, some long-term Teachable users report that analytics became limiting as their business grew, including difficulty filtering learners enrolled in several courses. Some Thinkific users report the need for external tools for advanced reporting, and one reported completion data changing after course edits. These are individual reports, not measured results.

## Payments and selling

This is Teachable's strongest area. Every Teachable plan includes checkout with global payments and tax handling, coupons, memberships and subscriptions, upsells and abandoned-cart recovery; affiliate marketing starts on Builder ([Teachable, 2025](https://support.teachable.com/en/articles/11682410-new-teachable-plans-in-june-2025); [Teachable pricing](https://teachable.com/pricing)).

Thinkific's selling tools are close behind. Every plan includes coupons, bundles, order bumps, payment plans, memberships, free trials, abandoned-cart emails and affiliate reporting. PayPal, group orders and invoicing start on Grow ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)).

The difference is emphasis. Teachable presents tax handling as part of the product. On Thinkific, check exactly how tax is collected and remitted for your country. For Australian sellers on either platform, confirm GST treatment, supported payment processors and who handles refunds and chargebacks before you launch.

Customer reviews include a serious payment failure reported by one Teachable user during a large sales campaign, and early problems with Thinkific Payments reported by another. Treat these as reasons to test your own checkout, not as evidence of how often either fails.

## Marketing and websites

Both include a site builder, landing pages and custom domains. Thinkific lists email automation, advanced CSS and HTML editing and analytics integrations on every plan, with bulk email to students and removal of Thinkific branding from Grow ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)). Teachable lists built-in email marketing among its platform features and editable abandoned-cart emails on Growth. [TO VERIFY: which Teachable plans include built-in email marketing]

For a single course or a small catalogue, either is enough. For several products, funnels and cohorts, expect to connect an external email or CRM tool to either platform. Our guide to [creating an online course that sells](/guides/how-to-create-an-online-course-that-sells/) covers the marketing side.

## Communities

Older comparisons say Teachable is weak on community. That's out of date: Teachable now includes communities with unlimited spaces on every plan ([Teachable, 2025](https://support.teachable.com/en/articles/11682410-new-teachable-plans-in-june-2025)).

Thinkific includes one community with 10 spaces on Start, three on Grow and 10 on Expand, plus in-course discussions on every plan ([Thinkific pricing](https://www.thinkific.com/pricing/)). Its communities include paid memberships, live events, threaded replies and notifications.

For a cohort course where discussion sits inside the lessons, test the setup you need on the plan you'd buy.

## SCORM and authoring tools

If you build in Articulate, Captivate or another authoring tool, this section may decide it for you.

**Thinkific** supports SCORM on Thinkific Plus only, its quoted enterprise tier ([Thinkific support](https://support.thinkific.com/hc/en-us/articles/360030376274)), with completion, pass/fail and score tracking. Its Articulate integration is also a Plus feature. [TO VERIFY: that a SCORM package runs as a standalone course rather than a lesson, the 1,024 MB package limit, and SCORM 1.2 and 2004 4th edition support, against Thinkific's SCORM help article]

**Teachable** doesn't document SCORM, xAPI or cmi5 support. It is built for courses created inside the platform.

For most creators this doesn't matter. For training providers with a library of SCORM packages, it means Thinkific Plus or a different kind of platform.

## Accessibility, security and data

These questions rarely appear in platform comparisons, but they matter for associations, training providers and anyone selling to institutions.

**Accessibility.** Teachable's accessibility statement says it is "partially conformant with WCAG 2.1 level AA", based on self-evaluation, keyboard testing and VoiceOver testing. It was last updated on 12 October 2021, so it isn't evidence of the current product ([Teachable accessibility](https://teachable.com/accessibility)). Thinkific's statement, reviewed in April 2026, says it is working towards WCAG 2.1 AA, has engaged an independent specialist to audit its learner experience, tests with disabled participants through Fable, and acknowledges that some admin and creator areas are still being updated ([Thinkific accessibility](https://www.thinkific.com/accessibility/)). Neither has published a current accessibility conformance report (a VPAT or ACR). Thinkific's is the more detailed and recent disclosure, but neither statement establishes full conformance.

**Security.** Teachable states that it holds a SOC 2 Type II accreditation, audited annually, with the report available under NDA ([Teachable security](https://www.teachable.com/security)). Thinkific states that data is encrypted with TLS 1.2 or later in transit and AES-256 at rest, that third parties run penetration tests quarterly, and that its practices meet SOC 2 standards for security on Thinkific Plus ([Thinkific security](https://www.thinkific.com/security/)). These are vendor statements. If you need assurance, ask for the reports and check the audit period and what they cover.

**Where data lives.** Thinkific states that it "hosts customer and learner data in the United States" ([Thinkific security](https://www.thinkific.com/security/)). Teachable's public security page doesn't say where data is stored. Neither offers Australian data residency that we could find. For Australian organisations handling learner information, that's a question to raise under the Privacy Act 1988 before you sign.

## How much creators sell on each platform

Both companies publish how much money passes through their platforms, but not in the same way, so read this chart carefully.

Thinkific, a listed company, reports gross merchandise volume (GMV) every year: the total value of transactions on its platform. It grew from US$276.4 million in 2020 to US$414.8 million in 2021, then levelled off at about US$460 million a year in 2024 and 2025 ([Thinkific, 2026](https://investors.thinkific.com/2026-03-05-Thinkific-Announces-Fourth-Quarter-and-Full-Year-2025-Financial-Results)). Teachable said its creators "earned close to $400 million" in 2020 and passed US$1 billion in all-time sales that year ([Teachable, 2021](https://teachable.com/blog/2020-teachable-creator-stats-data)). Since then it has reported only combined figures with its parent company, Hotmart.

These are totals across thousands of creators. They say how big each marketplace is, not what a typical creator earns. Neither company publishes a median.

## What customers say

On Capterra's comparison page, Thinkific rated 4.4 out of 5 overall and Teachable 4.2, with Thinkific ahead on ease of use, value for money, functionality and customer service. The widest gap was value for money: 4.2 for Thinkific against 3.7 for Teachable ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/compare/Teachable-vs-Thinkific/), October 2026). [TO VERIFY: Capterra ratings and review counts, which were 185 for Teachable and 208 for Thinkific in our research notes]

The themes are consistent across review sites. Teachable users praise its simple setup and selling tools, and raise price increases, the Starter transaction fee and support responsiveness. Thinkific users praise its course structure and learner engagement, and raise plan restrictions, reporting limits and difficulty exporting content in bulk. Review-site ratings are self-selected and not a controlled measure, so read them as a signal, not a score.

## What we'd do

If you're choosing now, list the three things your course can't work without, then find the cheapest plan on each platform that includes all three. For many creators that will be Teachable Builder. For anyone who needs certificates and assignments, it will usually be Thinkific Start. For SCORM, exams or single sign-on, it's Thinkific Plus or a dedicated LMS.

Then use the free trials to build one real module on each, with a quiz, a certificate and a test purchase, and check it on a phone. Our [online course design checklist](/guides/online-course-design-checklist/) is a good test plan for that module.
