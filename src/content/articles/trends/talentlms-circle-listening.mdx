Earlier this month, while most of the learning technology industry was busy announcing the next AI feature, or preparing their end-of-year recaps, TalentLMS did something that rarely gets labelled innovation. It brought a group of its customers to Athens for two days and asked them where the product should go next.

The event, TalentLMS Circle, ran on 11 and 12 November and was the first of its kind for the company. According to Anthony Reilly, Head of Learning & Talent Development at EIDA Solutions, TalentLMS first approached him about it in June. The stated aim was to give customer advocates from around the world a genuine seat at the table and an inside look at where the platform is heading. Anthony sat down for a fireside chat with TalentLMS's Alexandra Fotiadou about his company's journey with the platform and where L&D is going next, with AI the focal point.

We contacted Anthony afterwards. He told us:

> "TalentLMS Circle was a truly enjoyable experience. It was amazing to get to peek behind the curtain and get face time with all the amazing people at TalentLMS. It was fantastic to meet other learning professionals, see how they use TalentLMS, and learn a great deal from their approaches. I also learned a lot about TalentLMS, their plans for the future, and how customer-centric their approach is. It was a wonderful event and I hope to attend more in the future."

TalentLMS hasn't published a press release about Circle. We discovered the event through [Anthony's public LinkedIn post](https://lnkd.in/p/g5H6BMKk), which TalentLMS reshared.

On the surface it's a customer event, and every SaaS company runs those. But the timing, and the framing, say something important about where eLearning is heading.

## Building is cheap now. Knowing what to build isn't.

TalentLMS has shipped at a serious pace in 2026. Version 7.0, announced in June, introduced [Learning Playground](https://www.epignosishq.com/?p=1937), a private AI-powered space where learners rehearse conversations, work through decisions and build skills at their own pace. Version 7.2 in September added [AI avatar video](https://www.talentlms.com/blog/talentlms-september-2026-release/) that turns a script, or existing course material, into a presenter-style video. It also lets HR events in its sister product TalentHR (onboarding, promotions, compliance deadlines) trigger training automatically, and lets learning paths assign the next course without an admin lifting a finger. Chief Product Officer Andrea Lorenzon has framed the direction as the shift from completion to capability.

Here is what might be uncomfortable for the industry. When AI can draft a course in minutes and generate a presenter in seconds, features become cheap to build and easy to copy. We [looked at AI course creation across the main course platforms](/trends/ai-course-creation-course-platforms/) earlier this year, and the pattern is already clear: AI course creators, AI coaches and avatar video are on their way to becoming standard. In this climate, the feature list stops being a moat.

What can't be generated is an accurate picture of what learners and L&D teams actually need. That only comes from listening, and from shifting your initial focus from engineering to understanding.

## The data says people aren't telling us what they don't know

TalentLMS's own research makes the case better than a keynote. Its [Learning Debt Report](https://www.talentlms.com/wp-content/uploads/documents/TalentLMS-Learning-Debt-Report-2026.pdf), published in July, found that 47% of employees have stayed quiet about not knowing how to do something at work. 28% say their manager doesn't know how often they struggle. And 37% say AI has made them look more competent than they really are.

These numbers come from an online survey of 1,200 full-time US employees aged 25 to 64, run in June 2026 by TalentLMS, which sells training software. Treat those numbers as a snapshot taken from one viewpoint, not a global census.

So what does that mean? If people hide their gaps from their own managers, your dashboards won't show you the problem. Completion rates, logins and quiz scores aren't necessarily lying. They might just be silent. In an AI-assisted workplace, a perfect score can be as much a warning sign as a failed one, which is why [formative assessment](/library/formative-assessment/) that people can fail safely matters more, not less.

The only way to find what's missing is to ask people, and to build places where they feel safe enough to speak honestly. Learning Playground is a product built on that insight: people need somewhere private to not know what they are building yet. It's that space at the table between friends and colleagues, where innovation is born. Circle applies the same idea one level up, to the relationship between a vendor and its customers.

## Listening at scale is hard. Just ask Instructure.

TalentLMS isn't the first platform to try to put customers at the centre of its roadmap, and the history is instructive.

Instructure has long run idea conversations in its Canvas community, where any registered member can propose a feature and product managers review the submissions. It's open, democratic and enormous. In a [post on its product blog](https://community.canvaslms.com/t5/The-Product-Blog/We-re-Listening-Part-2-Product-Roadmap-amp-Idea-Conversations/m-p/544527) in November 2022, Instructure conceded the system was not structured to aggregate and act on contributions at scale, and one community member pointed to 4,393 ideas sitting in open status.

Alongside that open forum, Instructure also takes part in something much smaller: the [NET+ Canvas Service Advisory Group](https://internet2.edu/bridging-the-gap-the-internet2-net-instructure-canvas-service-advisory-group-fosters-collaboration-and-impact/), convened through Internet2. It meets monthly and gives research and education institutions, often under a non-disclosure agreement, a direct voice in roadmap discussions.

The lesson is that open forums capture volume, while small rooms capture depth. You need both. But the small room is where you find out *why* people want what they want, and why is what turns a feature request into a better product.

## The catch: who gets into the circle?

Now for the provocation. Circle invited customer advocates, people who by definition already like the product. Listening to your fans is pleasant and genuinely useful. It is not the same as listening to the admin who churned last quarter, the learner who never logged in a second time, or the frontline worker for whom the mobile app is something you have to learn mid-shift.

Advocate events also do double duty as marketing. The warm LinkedIn posts are part of the value. That isn't a criticism. It's a design question, and it's one every learning team faces too.

The real test of whether a vendor is listening isn't the event. It's what changes in the roadmap afterwards, and whether customers are told what was heard and what was done about it. We'll be watching the next TalentLMS release with that in mind, and we'd love to see both champions and challengers at the next Circle.

## What this means for the rest of us

For learning designers and L&D teams, none of this is new. It's the oldest rule in instructional design: needs analysis comes before the solution, the first phase of the [ADDIE model](/guides/addie-model/). AI just makes it dangerously easy to skip. Before you prompt a tool to build a course, sit with the people who will take it, and [pilot it with a small group](/guides/how-to-pilot-an-online-course/) before you roll it out.

- **Run a listening sprint before any build.** Five honest conversations with learners and their managers will tell you more than a stakeholder brief.
- **Make it safe to say "I don't know."** Anonymous pulse questions, private practice spaces and low-stakes rehearsal surface the gaps people hide.
- **Read the silence in your data.** Low completion isn't the only signal. Perfect scores and fast finishes deserve a second look in an AI-assisted workplace.
- **Close the loop.** Tell people what you changed because of what they said, and use [feedback that people can act on](/library/effective-feedback/) in both directions.
- **Invite the critics, not just the champions.** The people who walked away know things your fans don't.

## The verdict

AI is making everyone in eLearning faster. But speed only helps if you're pointed in the right direction. TalentLMS's Athens experiment is small, and it should be judged by what ships next, not by the photos. The instinct behind it is right, though. In the next phase of digital learning, the winners won't be the platforms that generate the most. They'll be the ones that hear the most.
